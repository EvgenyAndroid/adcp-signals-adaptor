# HoldCos Bought the Fuel, Not the Referee

**Research current to 5 October 2026.** The white paper should argue that the six holding companies have spent 2025–26 buying the two things agents need — identity substrates (Acxiom, Epsilon/Lotame/LiveRamp, InfoSum/Choreograph, Merkury) and decisioning engines (Omni, WPP Open, CoreAI/Marcel, dentsu.Connect, Converged.AI, The Machine) — while nobody, including the protocols, has shipped the layer that makes an agent-to-agent media buy defensible to the client: a signed, portable record of what signal was used, under what policy, with what consent basis, at what price, and who answers for it. That gap is now a commercial problem, not a technical one. The ANA reports that **58% of marketers used principal media in the past year while 90% doubt whether the recommendation served their interest** ([ANA](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2026-03-principalmedia)); Omnicom is the only HoldCo to have stated on an earnings call that it executed live client buys agent-to-agent on AdCP ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media-buying/that-is-an-objective-omnicom-tests-ai-agents-to-cut-out-the-ad-tech-middlemen/)); the public AdCP registry held **30 agents on 23 August 2026, of which one was a buying agent** ([adcpexplorer](https://adcpexplorer.com/guides/adcp-registry-measured/)); and IAB Tech Lab's rival AAMP 3.0 is a draft with its governance chairs still marked "TBD" ([IABTechLab/OpenProposal](https://github.com/IABTechLab/OpenProposal/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.md)). The recommended paper therefore leads with receipts rather than agents: it reconstructs what a HoldCo data council actually grades on (DTS labels, consent and jurisdiction flags, clean-room fit, log-level delivery proof), shows the fee-stack and principal-media economics that make unverified agentic buying a reputational liability, and uses the author's live AdCP 3.1 signals agent (57/57 applicable storyboard scenarios on compliance line 3.1.20, RFC 9421-signed webhooks, DTS v1.2 labels with policy attestations) as the worked example of what a receipt contains. One caveat governs everything below: the research session's web proxy blocked nearly every trade-press and investor-relations page, so most figures outside the AdCP, OpenProposal, IAB and ANA PDFs and the local repository were taken from search-result summaries; Section 8 lists every such claim that must be checked against its primary before it is quoted.

## AdCP ships code every week, AAMP ships drafts, and the platforms ship MCP instead of either

The standards picture is sharper than the trade-press "standards war" framing suggests, because only one side has a running conformance program. AdCP 3.2 went generally available as release 3.2.1 on 30 September 2026 (wire pin "3.2"; 3.2.0 was never published), 3.2.2 followed on 4 October, and the 3.0.x and 3.1.x lines were patched the same week ([adcp roadmap](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/reference/roadmap.mdx); [GitHub Releases](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/releases)). The 3.2 line grew the manifest from 64 to 78 tools, 21 to 31 specialisms and 648 to 1,017 JSON Schema files, added compact lifecycle tasks (`request_proposals`, `accept_proposal`, `control_media_buy`), an experimental clean-room `audience_activation`, and "signal-quality attestations added to governance checks", "signed authorization binding for monetary commitments" and "immutable audit readback for governance outcomes" ([whats-new-in-3-2](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/reference/whats-new-in-3-2.mdx); [CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/main/CHANGELOG.md)). The v2 line was fully deprecated on 1 August 2026, and **4.0, targeted for early 2027, makes RFC 9421 request signing mandatory on every spend-committing operation** ([versioning](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/reference/versioning.mdx)). What AdCP still does not standardize is exactly the HoldCo back office: the 3.3 working-group cycle lists payment terms, FX, fees, settlement, disputes, credits and makegoods, acts-for delegation, and offline channels as open workstreams, plus a "production evidence program" that asks adopters to contribute "opt-in, redacted observations that distinguish sandbox, transaction, delivery, and reconciliation" — an admission that machine-observed adoption data does not yet exist publicly ([roadmap](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/reference/roadmap.mdx)).

Governance has matured but remains thin on the buy side. AgenticAdvertising.org is a Delaware 501(c)(6) founded in 2025, seeded by a Scope3 loan "repaid from membership revenue", with Brian O'Kelley still "AdCP lead architect" ([faq](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/faq.mdx)). Its elected founding board, announced around 28 September 2026 with 22 of 40 seats filled, is presided over by Triton Digital's Benjamin Masse and includes WPP Media CEO Ingmar Zach, Horizon Media and Butler/Till on the agency side ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/agentic-advertising-trade-group-adcp)). **No HoldCo appears in CONTRIBUTORS.md**, where Omnicom, Dentsu and WPP show up only as illustrative examples in the docs ([CONTRIBUTORS.md](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/CONTRIBUTORS.md)). The public registry, measured independently, listed 30 agents on 23 August 2026 — 25 sales, 3 signals, 1 creative, 1 buying — of which only 9 answered `tools/list` publicly; public visibility requires a paid tier, so the count understates enrolment but the sell-side skew is real ([adcpexplorer](https://adcpexplorer.com/guides/adcp-registry-measured/); [registering-an-agent](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/registry/registering-an-agent.mdx)).

IAB Tech Lab's AAMP is a different animal: a retrofit onto OpenDirect, AdCOM, OpenRTB and the Deals API, named on 26 February 2026, given transaction-ready buyer and seller SDKs in AAMP 2.0 in April, and extended on 22 September 2026 with AAMP 3.0's OpenProposal, whose README says "This is a draft. Nothing here is a ratified IAB Tech Lab standard", whose machine-readable schemas "are not published with this first draft", and whose governance file lists working group, chairs, editors and decision rule as "TBD" ([OpenProposal README](https://github.com/IABTechLab/OpenProposal/blob/main/README.md); [GOVERNANCE.md](https://github.com/IABTechLab/OpenProposal/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.md)). AAO's own FAQ states the two "use different primitives and are not wire-compatible merely because both support MCP and A2A" ([faq](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/faq.mdx)), and IAB Tech Lab's COO counted 13 overlapping functions in August ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/buyer-agent/)). The buy-side split is therefore a layering rather than an allegiance: transactions are happening on AdCP (Omnicom, Butler/Till, MiQ via Magnite, Havas via PubMatic), while the TV establishment convened by WPP Media does standards work with IAB Tech Lab and Prebid — and WPP sits on the AAO board at the same time, hedged across both.

The walled gardens and The Trade Desk have chosen a third path: official MCP servers that expose proprietary tools, not AdCP tasks. Meta shipped an MCP server with 29 write-capable tools on 29 April 2026 ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/meta-ads-ai-connectors)); Google's Ads MCP server (28 April) is read-only; Microsoft's Advertising MCP pilot (17 June) is read-only ([ppcnewsfeed](https://ppcnewsfeed.com/ppc-news/2026-06/microsoft-advertising-mcp-server-expands-open-pilot/)); The Trade Desk routes external models through its Open Agentic Kit and Koa Agents rather than adopting AdCP ([The Current](https://www.thecurrent.com/marketing/ai-stagwell-pilot-the-trade-desk-agentic-capabilities-koa-agents)); Amazon Ads Agent merges the DSP and console under native automation ([Amazon Ads](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/news/amazon-ads-agent)). Only Yahoo DSP, an AdCP founder, launched an open Agent Network (19 June 2026, 23 partners) ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415910/yahoo-launches-network-for-ai-agents.html)). Scope3, the protocol's most visible buyer-agent operator, became Apostra in late September 2026, down to roughly 40 people, handling about $1.2m of agentic buys and claiming no transaction fees ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/418337/scope3-renamed-refocused-on-agentic-media-buying.html); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/scope3-becomes-apostra-a-40-person-bet-on-ai-agents-that-trade-ads/)). The quantified pilot results all come from SSP-published single campaigns: Butler/Till and PubMatic reported **82% lower buy-side costs, 30% lower CPMs and 40% more impressions** for Clubtails in December 2025, with the saving framed as avoided DSP fees rather than lower publisher prices ([PubMatic](https://pubmatic.com/case-studies/butler-till-unlocks-greater-efficiency-and-performance-with-pubmatic-agenticos/)); Magnite and MiQ reported an 87% setup-time reduction with Scope3 as buyer agent ([Magnite](https://www.magnite.com/blog/why-magnite-built-a-seller-agent-and-what-it-signals-for-adcp/)). None is independently audited, which is the opening the paper should walk through.

## Data councils grade on declared metadata, and no HoldCo publishes its template

The procurement front door for a third-party signal is no longer the DSP data marketplace but the HoldCo operating system, and that changes what gets graded. A vendor must be matchable to the group's identity spine (Real ID, CORE ID, RampID, Merkury) or, for WPP, deliverable as ID-free aggregate signals into a model, and must work inside the group's clean room (InfoSum for WPP, LiveRamp for Publicis and Havas). Omni's audience agent "can review 10,000 different data attributes, create new audiences" from sources including Experian and PlaceIQ ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ai/how-omnicoms-ai-virtual-assistant-does-the-campaign-grunt-work-for-planters/)); Publicis's Microsoft Fabric agents will be "identifying valuable customer segments ... within pre-set guardrails" ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2026/04/08/microsoft-and-publicis-groupe-expand-their-strategic-partnership-to-power-the-future-of-agentic-marketing-for-businesses-worldwide/)). The consequence is that machine-readable labels — not segment descriptions a planner reads — are the practical gate, because an agent cannot grade prose. No public Omnicom, WPP Media, Publicis or IPG data-vendor questionnaire was findable, so the paper must present its rubric as reconstructed from the public gates those platforms and marketplaces rely on, not as a quoted HoldCo document. The repository's Tier-1 readiness plan claims its 25-criterion rubric was "drawn from published RFP templates from GroupM (Vendor Data Assessment 2024), Publicis Epsilon (Data Provider Qualification), IPG / Acxiom (Data Stewardship Review)" ([TIER1_READINESS_PLAN.md](/home/user/adcp-signals-adaptor/docs/TIER1_READINESS_PLAN.md)); the procurement researcher could not locate any of those documents publicly, so that provenance claim must be substantiated or softened before the paper repeats it.

The public gates are concrete enough to build on. IAB Tech Lab's Data Transparency Standard v1.2 (April 2024 "Privacy Update") carries fields for provider, taxonomy IDs, `audience_precision_levels`, `id_types`, `privacy_compliance_mechanisms` (GPP, MSPA), `data_sources`, `audience_inclusion_methodology` (Observed/Known, Derived, Modeled), refresh and lookback, and onboarder match keys, and its compliance program "requires an annual business audit" producing a label "for all in-market segments" ([README](/home/user/adcp-signals-adaptor/README.md); [IAB Tech Lab DTS](https://iabtechlab.com/standards/data-transparency-standard/)). After roughly five years the publicly announced certified roster is short — Alliant, Epsilon, Eyeota, with AdTheorent, Dynata and Anteriad referenced ([Alliant](https://alliantinsight.com/insights/alliant-strengthens-commitment-to-trust-and-transparency-with-neutronian-and-iab-tech-lab-certifications/); [Neutronian](https://neutronian.com/neutronian-announces-that-epsilon-has-earned-the-neutronian-iab-tech-lab-certification/)) — which makes certification a differentiator rather than table stakes, and the fact that Publicis's own Epsilon holds it suggests HoldCo-affiliated sellers treat the label as reputationally necessary. The Audience Taxonomy sits at v1.1 with ~888 rows across Demographic, Interest and Purchase Intent tiers and deprecates segment names describing sensitive types ([IAB Taxonomies](https://github.com/InteractiveAdvertisingBureau/Taxonomies/blob/main/Audience%20Taxonomies/Audience%20Taxonomy%201.1.tsv)). The AdCP Signals specification is the agentic layer's direct translation of these rules: providers declare `restricted_attributes` (GDPR Article 9 categories), `policy_categories` (`children_directed`), `consent_basis`, `art9_basis`, `countries`, `methodology`, `modeling.disclosure.jurisdictions[]`, `data_subject_rights.channels[]` with SLAs and `pricing`, and governed accounts must present a `governance_context` from a prior `check_governance` call that the signal agent "rejects activations with missing or fabricated" ([AdCP signals spec](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/signals/specification.mdx); [data-providers](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/signals/data-providers.mdx)). Notably the spec does not require providers to expose pricing, quality metrics or audience-size guarantees, which is itself a finding for a HoldCo reader. The reconstructed ten-point rubric the paper can defend, each line traceable to one of those gates, runs: provenance and methodology disclosure; consent and legal basis by jurisdiction; identity and cookieless readiness against the group's spine; clean-room compatibility; live activation destinations; refresh and recency; log-level delivery proof; audited label coverage; pricing transparency; and data-subject-rights routing with an SLA.

The ANA's benchmark series explains why those councils have become stricter and where they are heading. Open-web programmatic went from only 36% of spend reaching consumers in 2023 ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/ana-programmatic-advertising-report-MFA-crackdown/701705/)) to a working-media share of 47.1% on web and mobile in Q3 2025, with MFA down from 15% to 0.4%, **81.6% of spend through private marketplaces**, and the median marketer using 20 SSPs ([ANA Q3 2025 PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.mediapost.com/uploads/Q32025ProgrammaticTransparencyBenchmark.pdf)); by Q4 2025 disciplined advertisers converted 56.7% of spend into benchmark-qualified impressions ([ANA](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2026-02-programatic)). The one line item rising in every 2025 quarter is data: DSP data costs climbed from 1.6% to 2.7% of CTV spend and from 2.2% to 3.6% on web and mobile, and the benchmark now correlates log-level data with "data integrity and privacy risks" via Compliant's Publisher Compliance Index (median scores around 30) and recommends inclusion lists that exclude "domains not respecting data privacy regulations" ([ANA Q3 2025 PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.mediapost.com/uploads/Q32025ProgrammaticTransparencyBenchmark.pdf); [ANA Q1 2025 PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.mediapost.com/uploads/ANAQ12025Programmatic.pdf)). The ANA also notes "much of what is labeled 'curation' remains static" and that cost remains the primary buying incentive. A signals vendor should therefore expect a data council to ask for impression-level proof of segment delivery and privacy scoring, not a segment description — and should expect the ANA's principal-media contract language (disclosure, opt-in, audit rights, price transparency) to be imported into data-product reviews.

The regulatory floor beneath all of this moved in 2025–26. The Global Privacy Platform registry now defines 27 sections, with Maryland effective 1 October 2025 and Indiana, Kentucky and Rhode Island from 1 January 2026 ([GPP Section Information](https://github.com/InteractiveAdvertisingBureau/Global-Privacy-Platform/blob/main/Sections/Section%20Information.md)); state sensitive-data definitions are widening (Connecticut adds disability status and gender identity, Colorado adds neural data) and several states now ban targeted advertising to under-18s regardless of consent ([Digital Segment](https://www.digitalsegment.com/2026/07/21/state-privacy-law-patchwork-2026-what-it-means-for-your-marketing-stack/)); the EU DSA bans profiling-based ads using special-category data or aimed at minors while leaving an inference loophole for non-special data ([DSA Observatory](https://dsa-observatory.eu/2025/08/01/what-does-the-dsa-mean-for-online-advertising-and-adtech/)); and the Belgian Market Court on 14 May 2025 confirmed IAB Europe as joint controller only for the TC String while upholding the €250,000 fine ([IAB Europe FAQ, Jan 2026](https://iabeurope.eu/wp-content/uploads/20260108-FAQ_-APD-DECISION-ON-IAB-EUROPE-AND-TCF-Updated-January-2026.pdf)). For agent-selected segments the compliance unit becomes the segment-level declaration — jurisdiction list, Article 9 and state-sensitive flags, minors flag, consent basis, methodology — because the guardrails operate on declared metadata before price is even considered. UK and Canadian status, FTC data-broker enforcement and California AB 1542's enactment were not researched and remain gaps.

## Principal media nears a third of billings, and a protocol receipt does not expose the markup

The economic exposure has to be triangulated because no analyst has published a profit-at-risk number. Omnicom's integrated media discipline produced $3.15bn in Q2 2026, **52.5% of core revenue** ([BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/advertising/the-new-omnicom-makes-53-of-its-core-revenue-from-media-12207511)); its $4.1bn of 2025 "third-party service costs" revenue implies principal-media revenue "north of $2 billion" on an even split with experiential, though the company does not break it out ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/agencies/omnicom-investors-cheer-ipg-sell-off-despite-weak-ad-spend-in-q2/)), and management calls principal trading "part of the value equation" ([Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/principal-trading-is-part-of-the-value-equation-as-omnicom-media-arm-hits-us3-1b-931254)). Forrester expects principal media to reach nearly 33% of agency billings in 2026 with 81% of US B2C marketing executives planning to use more of it, and forecasts a 15% agency headcount cut in 2026 and 32,000 US agency jobs (7.5%) lost to automation by 2030 ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-truth-about-principal-media/); [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/press-newsroom/forrester-agency-ai-workforce-2030)). Two readings compete. Brian Wieser argues that mid-market leakage to Meta, Alphabet and Amazon's self-serve products — not agent substitution at large clients — explains most HoldCo revenue weakness, and that the 2026 bear case rests on three "overblown" misconceptions ([MediaVillage](https://www.mediavillage.com/article/brian-wiesers-outlook-on-agency-holding-companies-pivotal-research/); [Ad Age](https://adage.com/agencies/aa-brian-wieser-2026-agency-forecast-update-report/)); Barclays rated agencies "particularly at risk" should agents plan and execute autonomously ([Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/the-anthropic-effect-fear-of-ai-agents-trigger-major-saas-stock-sell-off/)). This is where the paper's central argument lives, and where the research is honest that it currently has no third-party evidence. The ANA's 2026 principal-media study found use up from 47% to 58%, concern about whether recommendations serve the client's interest up from 79% to 90%, only 57% of companies with guidelines and 63% with contracts that address it ([ANA](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2026-03-principalmedia)); its June 2026 transparency report named "agentic AI trading systems operating in supply chains with no contractual governance" as a new form of opacity and advised "Don't use your agency's AI to prove your agency's work — use your own, or an independent auditor's" ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415592/media-transparency-is-still-broken-heres-what-ma.html)). Nick Manning told the same ANA audience that "the big groups, in particular, are building black box agentic systems" and that agents talking to agents mean "we lose sight completely of where the advertising is going" ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415592/media-transparency-is-still-broken-heres-what-ma.html)). AdCP's designers claim the protocol "increases transparency compared to current programmatic systems by eliminating intermediary bid streams and obfuscating layers" ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/adcp-ad-context-protocol-agentic-advertising-standard/)), and 3.2 adds signed authorization binding and audit readback — but every current source describes transparency of auction and bid mechanics, not of agency resale margins. If the agency operates the buying agent, a protocol receipt does not by itself expose the markup; it only does so if the brand's agent, or an independent verifier, holds the transaction record. Fee stacking is the mirror risk: the author's own Part 3 essay records Scope3 pricing at 4% on routed and 8% on decisioned transactions and warns that "if agent fees stack on DSP + SSP + agency fees rather than displacing them, agentic trading has re-intermediated the very chain it promised to collapse" ([No Fluff Advisory](https://nofluffadvisory.com/writing/post-agentic-marketing-part-3/)) — a claim that now sits awkwardly beside Apostra's reported no-transaction-fee model and must be reconciled. Apostra's ~$1.2m of agentic buys against Omnicom's ~$3.1bn quarterly media revenue is the scale gap the paper should state plainly. The HoldCo counter-case has four documented pillars — proprietary identity, cross-platform orchestration that no single walled-garden agent can do, client demand for governance, and the growth of Publicis and Stagwell — but no independent analyst evidence that clients pay a premium for data gravity, and the WFA's 66% rise in on-site implant teams suggests clients value people embedded in their governance more than HoldCo data assets.

## Recommended thesis, structure and evidence plan

The thesis should be stated in one sentence the CEO can repeat and one paragraph the CTO can defend. The sentence: *the holding companies have bought the fuel and the steering for agentic buying, and the next thing they will be asked to produce is the receipt.* The paragraph: in 2026 every HoldCo owns an identity spine and a decisioning OS, one has transacted agent-to-agent on an open protocol, and clients are buying more principal media while trusting it less; agent-to-agent trading can either deepen the black box that the ANA, Manning and Ebiquity already describe, or become the first media channel that ships with a signed, portable record of signal provenance, policy, consent basis, price and accountability. The protocols are halfway there — AdCP 3.2 has attestations and audit readback and will mandate request signing in 4.0, AAMP 3.0 is a draft object model — but no HoldCo has published a governance document for its agentic buying, and no protocol yet exposes the agency's resale margin when the agency runs the agent. The HoldCo that publishes receipts first converts a reputational liability into a trust product; the paper shows what the receipt contains, using a live, registry-verified signals agent as the worked example, and gives the data council a rubric it can score vendors and its own curated products against. The paper should declare where it sits: AdCP-native by the author's working-group seat, but judging both bodies "by what lands in the repository, not what lands in the feed".

The structure that matches both the circulating formats and the house style is a one-page executive summary with five numbered claims, followed by eight short chapters, each ending in a dated, falsifiable call, then the method and ledgers. Chapter 1 ("Six operating systems, one architecture") carries the HoldCo table above, refreshed after the 13, 20 and 29 October prints, with a buyer map per group (entry point, what they care about, proof required, common stall) in the playbook style. Chapter 2 ("Two stacks and three gardens") sets AdCP 3.2 against AAMP 3.0 by ancestry, maps the platform MCP servers, and states the registry census with its denominators. Chapter 3 ("What the data council grades") presents the reconstructed ten-point rubric, the DTS and AdCP field crosswalk, the ANA's rising-data-cost and privacy-scoring findings, and the 2025–26 regulatory floor, explicitly labelled as reconstructed from public gates. Chapter 4 ("The economics of the toll") triangulates exposure — media share of revenue, principal-media share of billings, Advantage+ run rate, Forrester's headcount forecasts, the WFA's 20%-versus-61% remuneration gap — and makes the fee-stacking and markup-visibility argument with its evidence status marked [Author inference]. Chapter 5 ("Anatomy of a receipt") is the paper's original contribution: the signal container's six parts mapped to concrete objects — DTS label, `policy_attestations[]`, `governance_context`, `idempotency_key` and `request_fingerprint`, RFC 9421 signature and JWKS discovery, `revoke_activation`, and a settlement close-out — each illustrated with a live sample from the repository and each marked by whether it is shipped in the spec, proposed, or absent. Chapter 6 ("Buyer agent or seller agent") gives a decision framework per HoldCo posture (operate your own buying agent, buy through an intermediary like Apostra, transact through SSP seller agents, or stay on platform-native automation) scored on autonomy, interoperability, fee stack and receipt custody. Chapter 7 ("Monday morning") is the procurement checklist in ANA safeguard style, including the implementer-seat test "show me your issues, not your deck" and the ten-question procurement test from Part 4. Chapter 8 ("Scenarios and calls") sets dated calls the paper can be held to: whether AdCP 4.0 ships mandatory signing in early 2027, whether WPP Media's video buyer agent reaches scale within its 6–9 month window, whether the LiveRamp deal closes by year-end and what rivals do next, whether any HoldCo publishes agentic-buy volumes by Q1 2027, and whether the two embedding vocabularies reference each other by end-2026. The method chapter, claim ledger, corrections ledger, source register and data downloads follow, with the evidence legend applied to every load-bearing sentence.

The evidence plan has four tiers. Tier A is primary material already read in full and safe to cite: the AdCP repository at HEAD `6adfebe` (spec, release notes, roadmap, charter, FAQ, contributors, registry docs), the OpenProposal and AAMP repositories, the ANA Q1 and Q3 2025 benchmark PDFs, IAB Tech Lab's Audience Taxonomy 1.1 and GPP section registry, and the local repository with its compliance state, security model, changelogs and workshop evidence. Tier B is the set of primaries that must be fetched before quotation because the session only saw summaries: the Omnicom Q1 2026 transcript PDF and Q2 8-K, the Publicis H1 2026 release PDF, the WPP interim RNS and transcript, the LiveRamp 8-K and DEFM14A, the Havas, Dentsu and Stagwell H1/Q2 releases, the ANA 2026 principal-media report and June 2026 transparency report, the WFA 30 September study, the Forrester and Gartner press releases, the AAO board announcement and member directory, the IAB Tech Lab AAMP 2.0 and 3.0 releases, the PubMatic and Magnite case studies, and the Apostra coverage. Tier C is data the author should generate himself, because no publication has it: a dated registry census (public and, via API tier, members-only) by agent type with `tools/list` reachability; a re-run of the federation probe measuring `adagents.json`, JWKS and signed-request adoption; attestation and `check_governance` coverage across listed agents; a fresh hosted-grader snapshot; and a re-scored Tier-1 rubric against the current build, since the only self-score on record is from April. Tier D is interviews or on-record quotes to seek from Yuvienco, Zach, Pretorius or Hulme, Schreurs and Manning, because none of the governance framing from the HoldCos could be read beyond headlines. Every figure in Tiers B and D carries the [Reported] tag until the primary is in hand.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the white paper's job. The obvious paper — "agents will disintermediate the holding companies" — is not supported by what could be verified: platform automation is capturing SMB and mid-market budgets at enormous scale, but enterprise disintermediation is a survey sentiment, and the only agent-to-agent spend on record is seven figures against quarterly media revenues in the billions. The less obvious paper — that the HoldCos' own economic engine, principal media, is colliding with a client governance apparatus (ANA benchmarks, log-level data mandates, WFA contract clauses) that is now extending from inventory to data — is where the evidence is dense, and it gives the author's held positions on referees, signatures and containers a commercial reason to exist rather than a philosophical one. Agentic buying does not threaten the HoldCo because it removes the agency; it threatens the HoldCo because it removes the agency's ability to say "trust us" without a record, at the exact moment clients have stopped accepting that answer.

What the paper can add that nothing in the 2025–26 field has is a measurement of its own: how many listed agents carry attestations, publish signing keys, honor governance checks and expose pricing, counted on a stated date with stated denominators, in the same way the author counted the registry in July. That number, plus a worked receipt from a live agent, is the differentiator against decks that quote 82% savings from a single unaudited pilot. The research also sets a hard sequencing constraint: the HoldCo chapter cannot be finalized before the three October prints, the LiveRamp close and the AdCP 4.0 security floor are the two external events most likely to move the argument before publication, and every figure that reached this report through a search summary must be re-read at the primary — which, given the house claim-ledger discipline, is not an afterthought but the method chapter itself.
