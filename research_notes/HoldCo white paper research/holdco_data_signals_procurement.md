## Q1. What do the HoldCo data platforms claim about third-party data onboarding (provider counts, labeling/scoring, governance gates, agentic planning)?

### Takeaway
Every HoldCo now positions a single "operating system" (Omni, WPP Open/Open Intelligence, CoreAI, Dentsu.Connect, Havas Converged) whose data foundation is an owned identity graph (Acxiom Real ID 2.6B IDs; Epsilon ~2.3B profiles + Lotame + pending LiveRamp; Merkury/Dentsu.Audiences) or, in WPP's case, an explicitly "identity-free" large marketing model fed by named data partners (Experian, Adstra, Circana, Adelaide, Lumen, retailers). Third-party data is framed as an input to these owned graphs/models and increasingly to agents that select audiences, but none of the retrievable public material discloses provider counts, segment-scoring methodology, or formal governance gates for third-party segments; the only documented quantified claims are identity-graph sizes and partner rosters.

### Inferences
- The procurement "front door" for third-party signals is no longer the DSP data marketplace but the HoldCo OS: a vendor must be matchable to the HoldCo's identity spine (Real ID / CORE ID / RampID / Merkury) or, for WPP, deliverable as ID-free aggregate signals into a model; and must be usable inside a clean room (InfoSum for WPP, LiveRamp for Publicis and Havas).
- The Omni "10,000 attributes" agent and Publicis's Fabric agents "identifying valuable customer segments … within pre-set guardrails" show that agents are already selecting segments, which makes machine-readable labels (DTS fields, taxonomy IDs, consent/jurisdiction flags) the practical gate, since a human planner is no longer reading each segment description.
- Publicis's promise that LiveRamp stays "independent" and separate from Epsilon, and Digiday's "Acxiom data dilemma" framing for Omnicom, indicate that neutrality of the identity/clean-room layer toward rival HoldCos is now an explicit evaluation issue for both buyers and vendors.

### Gaps
- No HoldCo publicly discloses how many third-party providers are onboarded, how segments are scored/graded, or what formal governance gates exist (the Omni, Open Intelligence, CoreAI and Dentsu.Connect pages were blocked; search summaries contain no such numbers).
- Could not confirm Dentsu.Connect rollout year, the Havas Converged third-party roster beyond LiveRamp/Epsilon/YouGov, or any Kinesso-specific post-merger integration detail.
- The Digiday "Acxiom data dilemma" article content (rival-HoldCo client concerns) could not be read; only its title/summary is available.

## Q2. What specific questions appear in HoldCo data/tech vendor questionnaires and RFPs?

### Takeaway
No public Omnicom/GroupM-WPP Media/Publicis/IPG data-vendor questionnaire or RFP template was findable (none surfaced in search, and paywalled trade coverage was unreachable). The best public proxies for "what a HoldCo data council grades on" are (1) the IAB Tech Lab Data Transparency Standard label fields plus its audit-based compliance program, (2) the Neutronian/IAB joint certification criteria, (3) LiveRamp's seller Data Policy and segment-information requirements, (4) the TAG TrustNet transparency requirements used by the ANA benchmark, and (5) the AdCP Signals spec's governance fields, which were designed so agents can grade a signal deterministically.

### Cited Findings
- **IAB DTS compliance program (what an auditor checks):** the program "is available to any organization that offers data – whether syndicated separately or bundled alongside with media – and is also open to adoption by data marketplaces"; completion "requires an annual business audit to confirm that the information provided within the labelling is reliable, that the organization has the necessary systems, processes, and personnel in place to sustain consistent label completion at scale, and that a label can be produced for all in-market segments available" **[search summary]** — [IAB Tech Lab DTS page](https://iabtechlab.com/standards/data-transparency-standard/)
- **DTS v1.2 label fields** (as implemented in this repo against the April 2024 "Privacy Update"): `provider_name`, `provider_domain`, `provider_email`, `audience_id`, `taxonomy_id_list`, `audience_criteria`, `audience_precision_levels` (e.g., Household), `audience_scope` (e.g., "Cross-domain outside O&O"), `audience_size`, `id_types`, `geocode_list`, `privacy_compliance_mechanisms` (e.g., GPP, MSPA), `iab_techlab_compliant`, `data_sources` (e.g., "Online Survey", "Public Record: Census", "Geo Location", "Web Usage", "App Behavior", "TV OTT or STB Device"), `audience_inclusion_methodology` ("Observed/Known" / "Derived" / "Modeled"), `audience_expansion`, `device_expansion`, `audience_refresh`, `lookback_window`, and onboarder fields `onboarder_match_keys`, `onboarder_audience_expansion`, `onboarder_device_expansion`, `onboarder_audience_precision_level` — [adcp-signals-adaptor README, "Data Transparency Standard v1.2"](/home/user/adcp-signals-adaptor/README.md) (local implementation; original spec at [iabtechlab.com](https://iabtechlab.com/standards/data-transparency-standard/))
- **Joint Neutronian–IAB Tech Lab certification:** announced late 2021 so data providers "can now simultaneously complete IAB Tech Lab's Data Transparency Compliance Program and Neutronian's data quality certification to streamline adoption of … Seller Defined Audiences"; Alliant was "the first-ever company to be awarded the new joint" certification; Epsilon and Eyeota (recertified Aug 2023, as a Dun & Bradstreet company) also hold it; AdTheorent, Dynata and Anteriad are referenced as certified **[search summary]** — [PR Newswire, Dec 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/iab-tech-lab--neutronian-partner-to-accelerate-data-transparency-and-quality-standards-for-the-digital-supply-chain-301435811.html); [Alliant](https://alliantinsight.com/insights/alliant-strengthens-commitment-to-trust-and-transparency-with-neutronian-and-iab-tech-lab-certifications/); [EIN Presswire (Alliant)](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/671178931/alliant-awarded-first-ever-joint-neutronian-iab-tech-lab-certification-for-data-transparency-standard-and-quality); [GlobeNewswire (Eyeota)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/08/22/2729585/0/en/Eyeota-a-Dun-Bradstreet-Company-Demonstrates-Superior-Performance-of-Quality-Privacy-First-Data-Through-IAB-Tech-Lab-and-Neutronian-Recertification.html); [Neutronian certification page](https://neutronian.com/certification/)
- **TAG TrustNet "Transparency Requirements"** (adopted by the ANA Benchmark; a model for supply-side vendor gating): "Verification and Identification: All participants need to be verified as legitimate legal entities and always identifiable by an ID provided by a recognized industry trade association. Data Access: All suppliers need to make a contractual commitment to provide ongoing access to impression LLD to any advertiser or publisher asking for it. … Data Fields: The LLD … needs to comply with specified data fields, including quantitative, qualitative, and financial information. Matching IDs: The impression LLD needs to include an ID (as defined by oRTB standards) to deterministically match impression LLD across suppliers. Data Matching: All parties involved in a transaction need to use an independent platform recognized by industry trade associations." — [ANA Programmatic Transparency Benchmark Q3 2025 (PDF), Appendix "TAG TrustNet LLD Register"](https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.mediapost.com/uploads/Q32025ProgrammaticTransparencyBenchmark.pdf)
- **AdCP Signals spec governance fields** (what an agentic buyer can grade on): `restricted_attributes` ("GDPR Article 9 special categories touched"), `policy_categories` (e.g., `children_directed`), `art9_basis`, `consent_basis` (GDPR Art. 6 bases/consent types), `countries` (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2), `data_sources` ("IAB Data Transparency Standard categories"), `methodology` ("observed, declared, derived, inferred, or modeled"), `modeling.disclosure.jurisdictions[]` (required when modeled), `segmentation_criteria`/`criteria_url`, `onboarder` ("required for offline/public-record sources"), `taxonomy.ref`/`taxonomy.values[]`, `refresh_cadence`, `lookback_window`, `data_subject_rights.channels[]` ("access/erasure/objection routing with SLA"), `pricing` (CPM and revenue share), `coverage_percentage`, `deployments[].is_live`, and a `governance_context` from a prior `check_governance` call that "signal agent rejects activations with missing or fabricated." Data providers "SHOULD declare restricted attributes and policy categories so governance agents can deterministically evaluate compliance." Providers publish definitions at `/.well-known/adagents.json` with `authorized_agents` so "buyers verify authorization by checking the data provider's domain directly." The docs do **not** require providers to expose "pricing terms, quality metrics, audience size guarantees, or specific destination/platform restrictions." — [AdCP signals specification (GitHub)](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/signals/specification.mdx); [AdCP data-providers doc](https://github.com/adcontextprotocol/adcp/blob/main/docs/signals/data-providers.mdx)

## Q6. Which 2025–2026 privacy/regulatory constraints most affect third-party audience signals used by agents (US states, sensitive data, EU DSA/DMA, UK, Canada)?

### Takeaway
The binding constraints are (1) US state laws that now cover 20+ jurisdictions with GPP sections (Maryland from 1 Oct 2025; Indiana, Kentucky, Rhode Island from 1 Jan 2026; Minnesota finalized), expanding sensitive-data definitions (Connecticut adds disability status and gender identity; Colorado adds precise geolocation and neural data), categorical bans on targeted advertising to under-18s regardless of consent (Connecticut and others), universal opt-out honoring in ~10 states, and California's AB 1542 proposal to ban sale/sharing of sensitive PI outright; (2) the EU DSA's ban on profiling-based ads using special-category data or targeting minors and the DMA's consent requirement for combining data across gatekeeper services; and (3) a TCF that survived litigation in narrowed form (Belgian Market Court, 14 May 2025: IAB Europe joint controller only for the TC String; €250k fine upheld; FAQ updated Jan 2026). The AdCP spec's `restricted_attributes` / `policy_categories` / `countries` / `consent_basis` fields are the agentic layer's direct response to these rules.

### Inferences
- For agent-selected segments the practical compliance unit is the *segment-level declaration*: jurisdiction list, Article 9 / state-sensitive flags, minors flag, consent basis, and DSR routing. Vendors that cannot populate these fields will be filtered out before pricing is even considered, because HoldCo guardrails (Publicis "pre-set guardrails," AdCP `check_governance`) operate on declared metadata rather than on reading descriptions.
- The EU inference loophole (non-special data used to infer sensitive traits) and Audience Taxonomy 1.1's deprecation of sensitive names together mean HoldCo reviewers will look at *methodology* ("Modeled"/"inferred") for sensitive-adjacent categories, not just at the segment name.
