## Key Question 1: What share of HoldCo revenue and profit is exposed to automation of media planning/buying, and what have analysts estimated (with dates and figures)?

### Takeaway
No analyst has published a single "X% of HoldCo profit is automatable" number that I could find; the exposure has to be triangulated. Media is now 52.5% of Omnicom's core revenue (Q2 2026), principal media is on track to be ~33% of agency billings in 2026 (Forrester), Meta's automated Advantage+ suite alone runs at a $75B annual revenue rate (Q2 2026), and Forrester forecasts agency headcount cuts of 15% in 2026 and 7.5% of US agency jobs (32,000) lost to automation by 2030 — while Madison & Wall (Wieser) argues the bear case is overblown and that mid-market spend leakage to Meta/Google/Amazon, not agent substitution at large clients, explains most of the revenue decline.

### Inferences
- Combining Omnicom's disclosures (media = 52.5% of core revenue; ~$2B+ principal media revenue in 2025) with Forrester's ~33%-of-billings principal media estimate, roughly half of HoldCo revenue and a disproportionate share of margin (principal media and data products carry higher margins than fee-for-service) sits in the activities that platform agents (Advantage+, AI Max/PMax, Amazon Ads Agent) are explicitly designed to automate. No analyst has put a profit-at-risk percentage on this; the white paper should present it as a triangulation, not an analyst estimate.
- The two competing explanations for 2025–26 HoldCo revenue weakness (agent substitution vs. mid-market leakage to platform self-serve) have different implications: Wieser's view implies the exposed revenue is already largely gone (SMB/mid-market) and large-client fees are stickier; the Barclays/Forrester view implies the large-client fee pool is next.
- Gartner's finding that in-house labor share rose (21.9% → 24.5%) while Typeface finds 60% cut agency spend is consistent with fee pressure flowing to agencies first rather than to brand headcount.

### Gaps
- No MoffettNathanson, Bernstein, Morgan Stanley or Goldman research note on HoldCo AI exposure was retrievable; searches for these surfaced only unrelated AI notes. The Barclays reference is second-hand.
- Wieser's "three misconceptions" (April 2026) content, Madison & Wall's HoldCo growth forecasts for 2026–27, and the MediaVillage/Madison & Wall earnings analysis quote's exact date could not be retrieved (Ad Age and Substack blocked).
- No source gives media buying's share of HoldCo *profit* (vs revenue), nor principal media margin rates; Omnicom's own disclosure is explicitly incomplete.
- EV/EBITDA for Omnicom, WPP, Dentsu, Stagwell was not obtained.

## Key Question 2: What did each HoldCo CEO say in 2026 about pricing models changing (FTE-based fees to outputs/outcomes) and about agents reducing headcount?

## Key Question 3: What is the documented state of the principal media debate in 2025–2026 and how does transparent agent-to-agent buying (protocol-level receipts, attestations) bear on it?

### Takeaway
Principal media is accelerating, not retreating: ANA's March 2026 study found 58% of marketers used it in the past year (up from 47%) while 90% now worry whether it is in their best interest and only 57% have governance guidelines; Forrester puts it near one-third of 2026 agency billings. Agentic buying standards (AdCP, launched Oct 2025, and IAB Tech Lab's 2026 agentic roadmap and transparency council) explicitly claim to increase transaction transparency by removing intermediary layers, but no source yet documents protocol-level receipts or attestations being applied to principal-media disclosure — that link is an inference, not a finding.

### Cited Findings

**ANA 2024–2026**
- ANA report "The Continued Acceleration of Principal Media (and the importance of proper governance)", released March 19 2026: 58% of marketers' companies used principal media in the past year (47% in the 2024 foundational study); 56% expect to use it in the coming year (41% earlier); 37% say use increased in the past year (24% earlier); 90% say their top concern is whether recommended principal media is truly in their best interest (79% in 2024); only 57% have company guidelines governing principal media; 76% cite reduced cost as the primary benefit — [ANA press release](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2026-03-principalmedia); [ANA report page](https://www.ana.net/miccontent/show/id/rr-2026-03-continued-acceleration-of-principal-media); [Ad Age](https://adage.com/agencies/media-agencies/aa-ana-2026-principal-media-study/); [Report PDF](https://d3q27bh1u24u2o.cloudfront.net/upload/2026-03/2026-ANA-Principal-Media-Report.pdfFINAL.nZb8gVLA.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2026 debate has shifted from "should principal media exist" to "is it governed": the ANA numbers (58% adoption, 90% concern, 57% guidelines) describe a practice that marketers are buying more of while trusting less.
